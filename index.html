Home monitoring after chemotherapy

Know when it’s time to call for help.

Seravyx watches heart rate, breathing, oxygen and temperature at home and turns them into one clear message for patients and their families: stable, check in, or get help now.

Join the pilot waitlist See how it works

Being designed with the oncology team at the National Cancer Institute, Maharagama.

The problem

Between cycles, nobody is watching.

After chemotherapy the immune system is weak, and an infection can turn serious within hours. Patients go home between cycles, and early warning signs such as a rising heart rate or a fever are easy to miss or explain away.

Our solution

A ward-style early warning, at home.

A chest patch and a finger clip measure vital signs all day. The app works out a NEWS2 score, the early-warning score nurses use on the ward, and tells the family in plain words what to do. If the score is high, it alerts the family and shows who to call.

How it works

  1. 1

    Wear it

    Put on the patch and the clip at home. The first reading arrives in about five minutes.

  2. 2

    It watches the trend

    Readings are checked every few minutes and scored with NEWS2.

  3. 3

    You know what to do

    Stable, check in, or get help now, with one button to call the right person.

Built for families, trusted by the ward

  • One clear answer

    No charts to decode. A status word, a colour and an icon, so it works for everyone.

  • Alerts that reach someone

    “Get help now” sounds even on silent and repeats until someone responds.

  • Family stays in the loop

    Link a caregiver with one code. They see the same status and alerts.

  • A report for the oncology team

    Share a two-page A4 summary of the week before the next appointment.

  • Sinhala, Tamil and English

    Large, readable text and a Night theme for checks after dark.

  • Works without internet

    Readings are saved on the phone, and alerts fall back to SMS.
